# Ethics & Legal Boundaries

This project is a hackathon prototype for SIH PS 26151. To keep it legal, safe,
and honest in front of judges, the team commits to the following boundaries.

## What this project does NOT do

- It does not scrape, crawl, or connect to real dark-web marketplaces or Tor
  hidden services operated by third parties.
- It does not attempt to deanonymize any real person or real threat actor.
- It does not store, publish, or demo any real leaked personal data.

## What this project DOES do

- All marketplace/relationship data is **synthetic**, generated by the team,
  or drawn from **academic research datasets** that are already public and
  intended for security research (e.g. DUTA, CoDA, or similar).
- The infrastructure-analysis pillar runs only against a **self-hosted mock
  Tor hidden service** that the team stands up and deliberately misconfigures,
  to demonstrate the detection technique without touching any real target.
- All wallet-clustering demos use either synthetic transaction graphs or
  publicly documented historical blockchain data, not live tracing of real
  individuals.

## Why this matters for the pitch

NTRO is an intelligence-adjacent sponsor. Judges are likely to ask directly
whether the system was tested against real dark-web infrastructure. The
honest answer — "no, by design, and here's why" — is a stronger answer than
either dodging the question or (worse) having actually done it. State this
plainly and early in the pitch.
